📚 Complete IGCSE Business Studies 0450 Core Theory

Expand each unit to master definitions, formulas, and key concepts for your exams:

Unit 1: Understanding Business Activity

• Needs vs Wants: Needs are essentials for survival; wants are unlimited desires.

• The Economic Problem: Scarcity (unlimited wants vs. limited resources of Land, Labour, Capital, Enterprise) leads to Opportunity Cost (the next best alternative foregone).

• Added Value: Selling Price minus Cost of Raw Materials. (Note: Added value $\neq$ Profit).

• Business Ownership: Sole traders, Partnerships, Private Limited Companies (Ltd), Public Limited Companies (PLC), and Franchises.

• Business Growth & Objectives: Internal vs External growth, stakeholder conflicts, and causes of business failure.

Unit 2: People in Business

• Motivation Theories: Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs, Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory, Taylor's Scientific Management.

• Financial & Non-Financial Rewards: Wages, salaries, commission, profit-sharing vs. job enrichment, rotation, enlargement.

• Organisation Structure: Chain of command, span of control, tall vs flat hierarchies, delegation, decentralisation.

• Recruitment & Training: Job description, person specification, internal/external recruitment, induction, on/off-the-job training.

• Communication & Legal Controls: Internal/external communication barriers, employment contracts, unfair dismissal, minimum wage.

Unit 3: Marketing

• Market Segmentation & Research: Grouping customers by age, income, lifestyle. Primary vs Secondary research.

• Marketing Mix (4Ps): Product (USP, brand image, packaging), Price (Skimming, Penetration, Cost-plus, Competitive, Promotional), Place (Channels of distribution), Promotion (Advertising, BOGOF).

• Product Life Cycle (PLC): Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Saturation, Decline + Extension strategies.

• E-Commerce & International Markets: Opportunities/threats of selling online and entering foreign markets (joint ventures, licensing).

Unit 4: Operations Management

• Production Methods: Job production, Batch production, Flow production.

• Lean Production: Kaizen (continuous improvement), Just-in-Time (JIT) stock control to minimize waste and holding costs.

• Economies & Diseconomies of Scale: Purchasing, financial, marketing economies lowering average costs vs communication breakdowns.

• Location & Quality: Factors influencing factory location, plus Quality Control vs Quality Assurance.

Unit 5: Financial Information & Financial Decisions

• Sources of Finance: Internal (retained profit) vs External (bank loans, share issues, overdrafts, microfinance).

• Cash Flow Forecasting: Inflows, outflows, net cash flow, closing balance. Managing short-term cash deficits.

• Break-Even Analysis: Fixed costs, variable costs, contribution, margin of safety calculations.

• Accounts & Ratios: Income Statements, Statements of Financial Position, Profitability ratios (Gross/Net profit margin, ROCE) and Liquidity ratios (Current and Acid test ratios).

Unit 6: External Influences on Business Activity

• Government Economic Policies: Economic growth, inflation, unemployment, taxation, interest rates, and exchange rates.

• Environmental & Ethical Issues: Sustainability, carbon footprint, externalities, pressure groups, and ethical business dilemmas.

• Globalisation: Multinational companies (MNCs), import tariffs, quotas, and trade benefits/drawbacks.

A* Exam Technique Masterclass

⭐ How to Score A* in Cambridge IGCSE Business Studies

Master the Assessment Objectives (AO1 to AO4) as required by Cambridge examiners:

AO1: Knowledge (30-50%)

Memorize exact definitions and business terminology. Keep answers direct and concise for 2-mark questions.

AO2: Application (20%)

Always weave case study details (business name, products, context) into your points to secure full marks.

AO3: Analysis (20-30%)

Use cause-and-effect chains: "Because of X $\rightarrow$ leading to Y $\rightarrow$ resulting in higher profit/costs."

AO4: Evaluation (10-20%)

For 12-mark Paper 2 questions, make a definitive choice between options and justify it using financial or operational reasoning.

💡 The "K - APP - AN" Formula for 6-Mark Questions:

1. Knowledge (K): State a clear advantage/disadvantage.

2. Application (APP): Connect it to the case business context.

3. Analysis (AN): Explain the financial or operational consequence.
