Question Set: BeanCraft Coffee (BCF)

🏢 Business Context:

BeanCraft Coffee (BCF) is a partnership that robs and sells specialty coffee beans to local cafes in Ho Chi Minh City. BCF uses batch production methods and employs 12 skilled roasters. Due to a surge in demand, the partners are considering expanding operations by opening a retail coffee shop or purchasing an automated roasting machine to increase output. However, cash flow is tight due to high seasonal raw material costs.

(a) Define 'partnership'. [2 marks]

AO1 Knowledge: A form of business organization where 2 to 20 owners agree to run a business together, share profits, and bear joint unlimited liability [2 marks].

Partial credit: Mentioning business owned by two or more people without mentioning profit-sharing or liability [1 mark].

(b) Identify two factors of production used by BCF. [2 marks]

AO1/AO2 Mark Scheme: 1 mark per valid factor applied to BCF context (1 mark x 2 = 2 marks).

  • Land: Raw coffee beans sourced from farms.
  • Labour: The 12 skilled roasters employed by BCF.
  • Capital: Roasting equipment used in the workshop.
(c) Outline two benefits to BCF of using batch production. [4 marks]

Mark Scheme: Award 1 mark for each relevant benefit identified (max 2) + 1 mark for application of each benefit to BCF (max 2).

Benefit 1 (Flexibility): BCF can easily switch production batches to roast different specialty coffee blends depending on cafe orders [2 marks].

Benefit 2 (Quality Control): The 12 skilled roasters can inspect coffee beans at each batch stage, ensuring high quality before supplying local cafes [2 marks].

(d) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to BCF of having cash flow problems. [6 marks]

Mark Scheme (per point): 1 mark for K (Knowledge), 1 mark for APP (Application), 1 mark for AN (Analysis/Consequence).

Advantage (if any - usually liquidity constraints force tighter control): Forces management to negotiate longer trade credit terms with raw material suppliers, preserving short-term cash [3 marks].

Disadvantage: Inability to pay expenses. BCF may struggle to pay wages for its 12 roasters or purchase seasonal coffee bean supplies on time, leading to production stoppages and lost cafe customers [3 marks].

Paper 1 Instructions & Information

  • Duration: 1 hour 30 minutes (90 minutes).
  • Total Marks: 80 marks (4 compulsory case study questions, 20 marks each).
  • Coverage: Unit 1 (Understanding activity), Unit 2 (People), Unit 3 (Marketing).
  • Assessment Objectives: AO1 Knowledge (30%), AO2 Application (30%), AO3 Analysis (20%), AO4 Evaluation (20%).

Exam Tip:

Always contextually apply your answers to the given case study business in parts (b), (c), (d), and (e) to earn full AO2 application marks!

Question 1 • Unit 1 Focus

GreenLeaf Furniture (GLF)

20 Marks

Business Context:

Tariq is an entrepreneur who founded GreenLeaf Furniture (GLF) as a sole trader. GLF produces handcrafted wooden dining tables and chairs using timber supplied by local sawmills. Demand for high-quality wooden furniture has risen sharply due to economic growth in the region.

GLF currently employs 8 skilled carpenters in a small workshop. However, Tariq faces scarcity of skilled labor and high timber raw material costs. To fund the purchase of a larger automated factory, Tariq is considering changing GLF's legal form of business organization to a Private Limited Company (Ltd). He is also evaluating two growth strategies: opening a second manufacturing site (internal growth) or acquiring a competing local carpentry business (external growth).

[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Official Mark Scheme Definition: The next best alternative given up / foregone by choosing another item [2 marks].

Partial credit: Giving up an alternative without specifying 'next best' or 'foregone' [1 mark].

Model Answer: Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative that is foregone when making a choice between competing options.
[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 1 Mark AO2 Application: 1 Mark

Mark Breakdown: 1 mark per valid method identified applied to GLF context (1 mark x 2 = 2 marks).

  • Creating a strong brand image for handcrafted timber tables.
  • Improving design/finishing of the wooden chairs.
  • Offering customized wood staining or carving services.
  • Providing free home delivery and assembly for customers.
Model Answer: 1. Offering custom carving and finishing services on timber furniture. 2. Building a premium brand image for handcrafted wooden tables.
[4 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks AO2 Application: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: Award 1 mark for each relevant benefit identified (max 2) + 1 mark for application of each benefit to GLF (max 2).

Benefit 1: Limited liability. Tariq’s personal assets will not be at risk if the furniture business incurs debts when purchasing automated machinery.

Benefit 2: Ability to raise capital by selling shares to private investors. This helps fund the purchase of the larger factory without taking on excessive bank loans.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks AO2 Application: 2 Marks AO3 Analysis: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme (per point): 1 mark for identifying problem (K), 1 mark for contextual application (APP), 1 mark for explaining the impact/consequence (AN).

Point 1: Diseconomies of scale due to communication barriers. As GLF hires more carpenters across multiple workshops, messages may become delayed, resulting in production errors or delayed furniture deliveries.

Point 2: Liquidity and cash flow problems (overtrading). Purchasing additional machinery and stocking up on large quantities of timber requires large upfront cash outflows before revenue from expanded sales is collected, potentially leading to working capital shortages.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 1 Mark AO2 Application: 1 Mark AO3 Analysis: 2 Marks AO4 Evaluation: 2 Marks

Evaluation Guidance: Compare both options in context and provide a clear, supported final recommendation.

Model Structure:

• Internal Growth: Slower and lower risk. Tariq maintains full quality control over handcrafted furniture standards and workplace culture. However, building a second workshop takes significant time and delays revenue generation.

• External Growth (Takeover): Provides instant market share and existing customer databases, along with trained carpenters and machinery. However, integration costs are high and clash of corporate cultures might occur.

• Recommendation / Conclusion: Tariq should choose internal growth. Because GLF’s reputation relies heavily on handcrafted wooden quality, acquiring a rival workshop risks inconsistent craftsmanship. Slower internal growth ensures quality control while maintaining Tariq's brand image.

Question 2 • Unit 2 Focus

Apex Logistics Ltd (ALL)

20 Marks

Business Context:

Apex Logistics Ltd (ALL) is a courier and delivery firm operating a tall hierarchical organizational structure. Managing Director Sarah manages 6 regional directors, who each oversee 25 delivery fleet drivers (a wide span of control).

ALL is currently suffering from high employee turnover and excessive absenteeism among its drivers. Drivers receive a basic time-rate wage and complain about repetitive work and autocratic leadership. Sarah wants to improve driver motivation and productivity. She is considering either introducing profit-sharing or adopting financial bonuses alongside non-financial methods such as job enrichment and increased delegation.

[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: The number of subordinates working directly under / reporting to a manager [2 marks].

Model Answer: Span of control refers to the number of subordinates who report directly to a manager or supervisor in an organizational structure.
[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: Award 1 mark per valid management function listed (Planning, Organizing, Coordinating, Commanding, Controlling).

Model Answer: 1. Planning (setting delivery targets and expansion goals). 2. Controlling (monitoring driver absenteeism and performance).
[4 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks AO2 Application: 2 Marks

Benefit 1: Reduced employee turnover. Motivated drivers are less likely to leave ALL, reducing recruitment costs for new drivers.

Benefit 2: Higher labor productivity. Motivated courier staff will complete delivery routes faster and with fewer parcel damage complaints.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks AO2 Application: 2 Marks AO3 Analysis: 2 Marks

Advantage 1: Reduces workload for regional directors. By letting drivers plan daily delivery schedules, directors free up time to focus on strategic logistics planning.

Advantage 2: Fosters driver empowerment and job satisfaction. Trusting drivers with route planning fulfills higher-level esteem needs (Herzberg), boosting morale and reducing absenteeism.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 1 AO2 Application: 1 AO3 Analysis: 2 AO4 Evaluation: 2

• Profit Sharing: Provides direct financial incentive (Taylor), linking company profit to driver effort. However, individual drivers may feel their daily efforts have little impact on overall annual company profit.

• Job Enrichment: Giving drivers more challenging roles (e.g., managing customer service issues or route planning) addresses motivators (Herzberg). It incurs no direct cash cost to ALL.

• Conclusion: Job enrichment is better for ALL. Courier driving is repetitive, so giving drivers greater responsibility over route management directly improves engagement without increasing wage overheads if profits fluctuate.

Question 3 • Unit 3 Focus

Nova Tech Smartwatches (NT)

20 Marks

Business Context:

Nova Tech (NT) designs advanced fitness smartwatches. NT targets active professionals aged 25–45 (demographic market segmentation). Sales of its original model, FitPulse v1, are entering the saturation stage of the product life cycle due to intense competition.

NT plans to launch a innovative new model, FitPulse Pro, with heart-rate tracking and GPS. The marketing manager is debating whether to use price skimming or penetration pricing. Additionally, NT plans to enter new foreign markets by expanding its direct-to-consumer e-commerce website.

[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: Dividing a total market into distinct sub-groups of consumers who share similar characteristics or needs [2 marks].

Model Answer: Market segmentation is the process of dividing a target market into smaller sub-groups of buyers who share common characteristics, such as age, income, or lifestyle.
[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: Award 1 mark per valid primary research method (Online questionnaires, Focus groups, Consumer interviews, Product testing panels).

Model Answer: 1. Online consumer surveys. 2. Focus groups with fitness enthusiasts.
[4 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 AO2 Application: 2

Strategy 1: Redesigning packaging or releasing new strap colors for FitPulse v1 to refresh brand appeal for active consumers.

Strategy 2: Lowering the retail price or bundling FitPulse v1 with free fitness apps to attract price-sensitive customers.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 AO2 Application: 2 AO3 Analysis: 2

Opportunity: Access to global mass markets without opening physical stores, significantly expanding potential sales of FitPulse Pro while lowering retail fixed overheads.

Threat: High international shipping and return costs, along with foreign exchange rate risks or customs duties that increase prices for foreign customers.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 1 AO2 Application: 1 AO3 Analysis: 2 AO4 Evaluation: 2

• Price Skimming: Setting a high initial price. Recovers high R&D costs quickly from tech-savvy early adopters who value GPS feature innovation.

• Penetration Pricing: Setting a low initial price to gain rapid market share in a highly competitive wearable tech market.

• Recommendation: Adopt Price Skimming. FitPulse Pro has high-tech differentiated features (GPS and heart-rate monitoring) targeting high-income active professionals who equate high prices with premium quality.

Question 4 • Integrated Units 1–3 Focus

FreshBite Foods (FBF)

20 Marks

Business Context:

FreshBite Foods (FBF) produces organic fruit snack bars. Having previously operated in a niche market selling to small organic specialty stores, FBF is expanding into mass consumer markets by negotiating shelf placement with national supermarket chains.

The Marketing Director proposes spending $150,000 on national television advertising and supermarket sales promotions ("buy one, get one free"). However, the Finance Director is concerned about cash flow and whether national TV ads are cost-effective compared to targeted social media marketing. Furthermore, FBF must choose between using wholesalers or distributing directly to retail chains.

[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: A very large market where a standardized product is sold to a large number of consumers [2 marks].

Model Answer: A mass market is a market for products that are produced in large quantities and aimed at a broad population of consumers with general needs.
[2 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 Marks

Mark Scheme: Award 1 mark per valid sales promotion technique (BOGO offers, Money-off coupons, Free tasting samples, Price discounts).

Model Answer: 1. "Buy one, get one free" (BOGOF) offers. 2. Free tasting samples in supermarket aisles.
[4 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 AO2 Application: 2

Benefit 1: Repeat purchases. Loyal customers will regularly buy organic snack bars, ensuring predictable revenues for FBF.

Benefit 2: Brand resistance to price increases. Loyal health-conscious consumers are less likely to switch to rival snack brands if FBF increases prices slightly.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 2 AO2 Application: 2 AO3 Analysis: 2

Disadvantage 1: Reduced profit margins. Wholesalers buy in bulk but expect price markups, reducing profit per organic snack bar sold.

Disadvantage 2: Loss of promotional control. Wholesalers may not display or promote FBF’s snack bars effectively in retail outlets, weakening brand image.

[6 marks]
AO1 Knowledge: 1 AO2 Application: 1 AO3 Analysis: 2 AO4 Evaluation: 2

• TV Advertising: Reaches millions of households, building widespread mass market brand awareness quickly. However, TV ads are extremely expensive ($150,000) and reach many non-snack buyers, wasting money.

• Alternative (Social Media & Supermarket Promotions): Social media advertising can target health-conscious snack buyers precisely at lower cost, while in-store BOGOF deals encourage immediate purchase at point of sale.

• Recommendation: TV advertising is NOT the best choice for FBF at this stage. TV ads are too expensive and risk cash flow problems. Allocating funds to targeted social media ads combined with in-store supermarket promotions will be more cost-effective.
