🌐 Syllabus 0450 Curriculum Design & Assessment Architecture

Based on official Cambridge IGCSE Business Studies standards for 2026.

Course Aims & Structure

Develops informed understanding of business activity across primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors, focusing on marketing, operations, finance, and human resources.

Components: All candidates take Paper 1 (Short Answer & Data Response) and Paper 2 (Case Study). Each paper is 1 hour 30 minutes, valued at 80 marks (50% weight each).

Assessment Objectives (AOs)

AO1 Knowledge (40%)[cite: 11] - Recall business facts, terms, and theories.

AO2 Application (20%)[cite: 11] - Apply knowledge to specific business case contexts.

AO3 Analysis (25%)[cite: 11] - Analyse business data and construct logical arguments.

AO4 Evaluation (15%)[cite: 11] - Make reasoned judgments and recommendations.

Track A • Comprehensive Syllabus Notes

📚 Complete IGCSE Business Studies Core Theory (Units 1–6)

Expand each unit to review academic summaries across all functional areas[cite: 10]:

Unit 1: Understanding Business Activity

• Needs, Wants & Scarcity: Unlimited human wants vs finite resources leading to opportunity cost[cite: 10].

• Added Value: Selling Price minus Cost of Raw Materials (Distinct from profit)[cite: 10].

• Economic Sectors: Primary, secondary, and tertiary classification and shifting trends[cite: 10].

• Enterprise & Growth: Sole traders, partnerships, private/public limited companies, franchises, and internal/external growth[cite: 10].

Unit 2: People in Business

• Motivation Theories: Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs, Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory, and Taylor's Scientific Management[cite: 10].

• Financial & Non-Financial Rewards: Wages, salaries, commission, bonus vs. job enrichment, rotation, and enlargement[cite: 10].

• Organisation Structure: Hierarchy, span of control, chain of command, delegation, and leadership styles (Autocratic, Democratic, Laissez-faire)[cite: 10].

• Recruitment & Training: Job description, person specification, internal/external recruitment, induction, on/off-the-job training[cite: 10].

Unit 3: Marketing

• Market Segmentation & Research: Demographic, psychographic grouping. Primary vs. secondary research methodologies[cite: 10].

• Marketing Mix (4Ps): Product lifecycle & extension strategies; Pricing strategies (Skimming, Penetration, Cost-plus, Competitive); Place (Distribution channels); Promotion (Advertising, sales promotion)[cite: 10].

• E-Commerce: Opportunities and threats of online trading for businesses and consumers[cite: 10].

Unit 4: Operations Management

• Production Methods: Job, batch, and flow production systems[cite: 10].

• Lean Production: Kaizen (continuous improvement) and Just-in-Time (JIT) inventory control to minimize waste[cite: 10].

• Break-Even Analysis: Fixed costs, variable costs, contribution, break-even output, and margin of safety calculations[cite: 10].

• Quality Control & Assurance: Methods of ensuring high standards of production[cite: 10].

Unit 5: Financial Information & Financial Decisions

• Sources of Finance: Internal (retained earnings, asset sales) vs. External (bank loans, share issues, overdrafts, micro-finance, crowdfunding)[cite: 10].

• Cash Flow Forecasting: Inflows, outflows, net cash flow, closing balance, and working capital management[cite: 10].

• Accounting Ratios: Gross profit margin, profit margin, ROCE, current ratio, and acid test ratio[cite: 10].

Unit 6: External Influences on Business Activity

• Macroeconomic Policies: Economic growth (GDP), inflation, unemployment, interest rates, and taxation[cite: 10].

• Environmental & Ethical Issues: Sustainability, externalities, pressure groups, and ethical dilemmas[cite: 10].

• Globalisation: Multinational companies (MNCs), import tariffs, quotas, and exchange rate fluctuations (appreciation/depreciation)[cite: 10].
